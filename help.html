What Is the Game of Life?

Conway’s Game of Life is a zero-player simulation created by mathematician John Conway in 1970. It is a famous example of a cellular automaton, where simple rules produce complex and surprising behavior.

The Rules of the Game

Each cell on the grid has 8 neighbors. During each generation:

  • Underpopulation: A live cell with fewer than 2 neighbors dies.
  • Survival: A live cell with 2 or 3 neighbors stays alive.
  • Overpopulation: A live cell with more than 3 neighbors dies.
  • Reproduction: A dead cell with exactly 3 neighbors becomes a live cell.

How to Use This Simulator

  • Click cells to toggle them alive or dead.
  • Select a predefined shape (block, blinker, glider) and click the board to place it.
  • Press Play to animate the simulation.
  • Use Next Generation to manually step forward.
  • Use Randomize to fill the grid based on density.
  • Use Reset or Clear to restart.
  • Change grid size, audio, themes, and speed using the controls.
